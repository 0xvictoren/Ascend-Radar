What we built

A safety terminal for every launch on Ascend

Ascend Radar watches every token launched on Ascend, the launchpad on Kinetiq's Elysium L2. Each launch gets a 0–100 risk score in which every point comes with a reason a buyer can check on-chain. Radar also tracks each token's progress towards Ascended status and pushes alerts when something changes.

The problem

Ascend has no bonding curve. Tokens launch straight into hook pools quoted in USDC, and the best ones go on to Ascended status, a HyperCore spot listing and HIP-3 perps. That path rewards real communities, and it also makes a fast launchpad attractive to the usual playbook: a dev who buys through hidden wallets, bundles of snipers funded from one source, and serial deployers who rug and relaunch.

Buyers can't trace funding graphs by hand in the seconds that matter. Ascend's own Ascended check looks for "concentrated or coordinated token accumulation", so the same analysis matters to the protocol too.

What Radar does

Explainable risk score

Weighted rules, no black box. Every deduction is a sentence such as "Dev-linked wallets hold 23% across 4 wallets" that points to wallets you can open in the explorer.

Linked-wallet detection

Groups early buyers that share a funder (up to 2 hops), pass tokens between each other, or buy near-identical amounts in the same 2-second window.

Dev tracking

Follows the creator, the wallets it funds or sends tokens to, and creator fee claims. It alerts as dev-linked wallets cross 20%, 50% and 90% sold.

Serial-deployer memory

When a token rugs, Radar remembers the deployer and its funders. Their next launch is flagged from its first block.

Ascended progress

Shows market cap, unique holders, volume and coordinated holdings against Ascend's published criteria. The thresholds are placeholders until Ascend publishes them.

Alerts everywhere

A live feed in the terminal, plus Telegram and Discord, for new pools, dev sells, high risk, whale buys, creator-role changes and watched wallets.

Built on Ascend's real contracts

Radar indexes Ascend's live deployment on the Elysium testnet (chain 99801). It reads the verified AscendLaunchpad contract and the PancakeSwap Infinity / Uniswap v4 pool managers behind it:

On-chainWhat Radar does with it
LaunchedNew token. The creator's atomic dev-buy in the same transaction becomes the creator's first buy.
Swap on an Ascend poolA buy or sell, attributed to the wallet that sent the transaction. A sell partly filled by the protocol bid (Filled) is merged in.
Token Transfer wallet → walletMoves the balance and links the two wallets. Pool, vault, treasury and contract legs are ignored so routers and staking contracts can't link strangers.
CreatorTransferredCreator-role handoff alert (e.g. to a fee router).
CreatorClaimedFee claim. Claimed tokens count as the creator's, so selling them counts as a dev sell.
ListingSetAsideThe creator reserves 20% of quote fees for a HyperCore listing (a positive signal).
Explorer historyWho sent each early buyer and creator their HYPE and USDC. Faucets, bridges and other very busy wallets are treated as hubs and never link wallets.

Only launchpads deployed by Ascend's own deployer are indexed; look-alike forks that emit the same events are excluded. Windows are wall-clock based for Elysium's ~240 ms blocks: early buyers are the first 10 minutes, and sniping is the first 5 seconds, which matches Ascend's sniper fee as it decays from 90% to 1%.

Everything is stored in SQLite as an ordered event log. A restart replays the history and resumes from the last indexed block. Radar has no runtime dependencies beyond Node.

Risk signals

A token starts at 100. Each rule that fires moves the score and adds a reason. Below 40 is high, below 70 is caution, and the rest is low.

A dev-linked group is scored once, as dev holdings, never again as a bundle. The creator's own dev-buy never counts as sniping. Positive signals are shown but don't count when any severe red flag (−25 or worse) is present.

Results so far

We indexed Ascend's entire testnet history and replayed it through Radar. The backtest labels come only from explicit on-chain facts (did the creator wallet itself sell out, and did the price collapse?), never from Radar's own clustering, so the test isn't circular.

Honest read: the testnet is mostly buy-only bot and load-test traffic with valueless mock USDC, so there are almost no dumps to predict. That leaves one labelled rug and 17 clean launches, too few to quote an accuracy number. We didn't tune any thresholds on this sample.

The two cases we do have are still telling. The one rug (BTC) was a 100% creator exit followed by a 99.6% price drop. Radar rated it caution before the dump and high only after. The same creator's next launch (FGK) was rated high-risk from launch as a serial deployer whose wallets hold 98% of supply. It counts as a "false positive" only because it hasn't dumped yet.

The same backtest runs unchanged on mainnet history. That result is the number that counts.

Pro & revenue

  • Free: the terminal, every score and reason, and the alert feed.
  • Pro subscription: copy-alerts on any wallet, plus the public risk API. You pay in USDC on Elysium: send the price to the treasury and paste the transaction hash. Radar checks the payment on-chain and issues a key, with no payment processor involved.
  • Pay-per-call API (x402): bots and wallets can call /api/v1/risk/:token without an account by paying per request in USDC through the x402 protocol.
  • Buy on Ascend: every token page links to the token on Ascend's app. Once Ascend publishes how referrals attach, those buys earn Ascend's 20% referral share of swap fees.
